The Pan American Health Organization announced Tuesday that the United States has lost the elimination status it won in 2000, after the virus spread uninterrupted for more than a year. Measles Is Officially Back in the United States. What Now? (#4)

Regaining elimination status will take years, because the metric requires twelve continuous months without sustained transmission — and the binding constraint is not time but coverage. Nine of every ten unprotected people exposed become infected. About 11 percent of this year's patients were hospitalized. "We're really in kind of a new Wild West of our own making," said Dr. Christopher Monson. Local health departments need resources; hospitals need capacity; the vaccination schedule itself may need revision.

Why Ebola Patients Avoid Hospitals, Spreading the Disease at Home (#6)

In eastern Congo, the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record is being sustained by a rational calculation. Gorette Mbusi had already lost a daughter who was taken to a hospital and died there. When health workers came for her 17-year-old grandson, she refused. Community transmission is the mechanism; distrust is the cause; and distrust was earned. Some villagers believe Ebola is a curse, some believe entering a hospital means imprisonment, and some have simply watched the system fail.
