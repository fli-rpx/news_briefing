Cluster 1: Geopolitics & International Affairs

Thematic Hook

Every American off-ramp was withdrawn this week — the Iran channel was reassigned, the Russia channel was handed to the Europeans, and the China channel turned out to run through a data-center lease.

Trump Pulls Vance Off Iran Talks (#6)

The president announced on social media that Vance's time is better spent investigating "Massive Corruption and Crime" in Democratic-run cities, adding flatly: "We don't need a deal with Iran." This came after Vance had already sent a letter through Qatari mediators to Gen. Mohsen Rezaei proposing renewed comprehensive talks. The mechanics matter more than the rhetoric: a working channel existed, staffed by the one senior official who had publicly blamed the war for the midterm rout — and it was withdrawn, not repurposed. What remains is a president weighing escalation, withdrawal, or the status quo, with no criterion for choosing.

In Moscow, Witkoff and Kushner Ask Putin to Include Europe (#7)

The envoys flew to the Kremlin on Thursday, then to Dublin to brief Zelensky at an air base with "planes for both delegations waiting nearby." Kushner's line — "We cannot conclude this deal without Europeans being at the table" — is an admission of a structural limit: the U.S. cannot deliver what Russia says it needs, which is a redrawn European security architecture. Russia is negotiating the postwar order before the war ends, which is the strongest seat at any table. Zelensky said Europe will join the next round; Moscow has not confirmed that.

A.I. Weapons Need Data. Ukraine Has a Lot to Offer (#8)

Kyiv opened Avenger Labs — roughly five million battlefield videos plus audio and text — free to allied defense firms, provided the Ukrainian military receives licenses to whatever software results. Twelve British companies are first in. This is the day's most coherent strategic move: Ukraine is trading its one surplus (labeled combat data, which nobody else can supply at volume) for its acute deficits (soldiers and interceptors). It is also mortgaging future advantage — the models trained on Ukrainian data will be sellable to anyone a decade from now. Separately, Zelensky said Starshield access was restricted again, a reminder that Ukrainian command depends on a foreign private company's goodwill.

Israel Strikes Nasser Hospital in Gaza (#9)

Two missiles hit the largest functioning hospital in southern Gaza, killing at least six — among them Dr. Rezeq Abu Shaira, 52 — and wounding 28 more. Israel says it targeted a Hamas commander "responsible for holding numerous hostages captive" and that Hamas ran a command center at the complex; Hamas calls it "a heinous crime targeting a civilian medical facility." The paper notes that residents have said Hamas-affiliated officials sometimes use Gaza's hospitals as offices — a fact that makes every future hospital strike easier to justify and harder to adjudicate.

Iran Seizes Dissidents' Assets Abroad (#10)

A retired couple's bank cards stopped working in May; their assets had been frozen by order of the intelligence ministry, punishment for their daughter's social-media posts from Europe. The Times interviewed 12 Iranians abroad whose property, or their families', was selected for confiscation this year. This is the state that Washington has decided it does not need a deal with.

In China's Spy Operations, Romance Can Be a Long Game (#2)

U.S. officials say the Ministry of State Security recruited businesswoman Alice Huang — a top executive at a Singapore data-center company that rents Nvidia-powered computing to Chinese firms — to collect intelligence on Jensen Huang. China calls the allegation a smear; no evidence has been published; Singapore says Washington has not meaningfully cooperated with its own inquiry into the company. The collection vector was commercial, not clandestine: proximity to chip allocation is proximity to intelligence.

Russia's Ambassador to the U.S. Dies at 66 (#29)

The death lands in the same week two American envoys were in the Kremlin asking Moscow to admit Europe to peace talks. The diplomatic channel between Washington and Moscow is now being worked by ad hoc envoys rather than by the embassy that is losing its principal, which is one more reason the Witkoff-Kushner shuttle is carrying more weight than any single back channel should.

Prediction

No senior-level U.S.–Iran negotiating session will convene before February 1, 2027. (Rationale: the designated envoy has been reassigned, the president has publicly declared a deal unnecessary, and Iran's incentive to wait rises as Washington's costs accumulate — a channel does not reappear without a staffer assigned to it.) Confidence: 72% (high confidence)

Cluster 2: US Politics & Domestic Policy

Thematic Hook

Democrats won a landslide and immediately split over whether it was a mandate for the center or permission for the left — a disagreement they can afford, for now, because the arithmetic is so much better than the argument.

At a Civil Rights Landmark, Jubilation Over Colom's Victory (#13)

Scott Colom will be Mississippi's first Black U.S. senator since Reconstruction — the third ever, the first two having been chosen by the state legislature. The party was at Tougaloo College, steps from the chapel where Fannie Lou Hamer and Medgar Evers spoke, eight days after the election, 63 years after Evers was killed in his driveway. A 21-year-old freshman's summary — "We've been waiting a long time... We back" — is the sentence of the day.

Blue Texas? Battleground Kansas? (#14)

Seven of Democrats' eight Senate flips came in states the party has not carried in a presidential race since 2012. They swept every statewide office in Texas, broke North Carolina's gerrymander, won two statewide races in Indiana, and lead narrowly in Alaska's uncalled race. They flipped House seats in districts Trump won by 15-plus points two years ago. Senator Raphael Warnock reads it as license to expand in the South; other Democrats want Ohio and Iowa back in play. The caution: a coalition assembled on affordability is not automatically a coalition assembled on ideology.

Democrats Now Have a Path to Sidestep the Electoral College (#15)

Eighteen states and D.C. are in the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact, representing 222 electoral votes. Tuesday gave Democrats trifectas in Arizona, Nevada, Michigan, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin — 61 more, for 283, past the 270 activation threshold. No amendment required. The binding constraint is governors: Nevada's legislature passed the compact in 2019 and a Democratic governor vetoed it, warning it would diminish small-state influence.

Democrats, Take the Deal (#31)

The editorial board's argument is the sharpest political advice in the paper. The Senate advanced permitting reform 73-25 on a procedural vote; the bill would make it cheaper to build solar farms, batteries and transmission, and therefore cheaper to power a home. Some Democrats want to scrap it and write a better bill once they control Congress in January. The board's reply: Trump would veto the follow-up, and a party that campaigned on affordability cannot refuse to lower energy prices on principle.

When Can We Stop Talking About Trump? (#32)

Frank Bruni and Bret Stephens stage the argument the party cannot resolve in public: whether a midterm won on affordability was a verdict on the president or a verdict on grocery bills, and whether the 2028 nominee should run against Trump or against the cost of living. Their exchange is the honest version of a debate every Democratic consultant is having privately — and it sits one page from the editorial board's plea to bank a concrete win now, which is the same argument in legislative form.

Shooting Spree by Doctor in Washington, D.C. (#16)

A 55-year-old emergency physician, angry about condo repair work, killed the building manager in his building's gym in Logan Circle, shot four people from his seventh-floor balcony, fired at police through his door, set his unit on fire, and killed himself. One victim is in critical condition. The building is less than a mile from the White House.

Cities Block ICE Jails by Running Out the Clock (#18)

Opponents of new federal detention centers are using procedure as a weapon — stretching zoning reviews, environmental assessments and permit hearings until contracts or appropriations lapse. San Antonio is the watched case. It works because federal procurement is impatient and municipal law is not.

Somali Bodyguard Indicted in a Bid to Incriminate Ilhan Omar (#19)

A former bodyguard for Representative Omar faces federal charges; her allies describe the case as an attempt to manufacture a basis for incriminating her.

Trump Hints at an Immigration Deal, Then Reverses (#20)

The pattern — open the door after a loss, close it again — has repeated across this presidency and now reads as a governing style rather than a negotiation.

Cluster 3: Business, Tech & Economy

Thematic Hook

A company worth more than most countries lost its founder in an evening, and the only thing that turned out to be diversified was the risk.

Jensen Huang Resigns From Nvidia (#1)

Huang stepped down Thursday after disclosures of an affair with an executive who reported to him and with a woman U.S. officials identified as a Chinese spy. A 10-member board met for several hours Wednesday evening, named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO, and appointed investor Harvey Jones chairman — committing that "operations, customer commitments and product road map" would continue uninterrupted. Huang, 63, founded the company in 1993 and had made it the premier maker of A.I. chips with an estimated 90 percent share of the market. President Trump had been told of the relationship with the woman believed to be a Chinese intelligence asset and took no action; he had publicly defended Huang as an "honorable man."

Nvidia Shares Tumble (#5)

The stock fell as much as 14 percent intraday before closing down more than 4 percent — nearly $1 trillion of market value gone this month alone — while the broader market rose slightly. "This is an Nvidia problem before it is an A.I. problem," said Siebert Financial's Mark Malek, who flagged the real contagion path: smaller A.I. cloud companies that "leaned on Nvidia's checkbook." The 10-year Treasury yield rose above 5 percent for the first time in weeks, meaning the bond market sold off on the same day as the equity shock.

Huang's Downfall Costs the A.I. Boom a Top Backer (#4)

Nvidia is not merely the dominant chipmaker; it is the industry's "central bank," financing customers and suppliers through complex deals. Brookings's Aaron Klein posed the question of the week: "Can one C.E.O.'s sex life take down the economy?" Almost certainly not by itself — but the company's 90 percent share means the turmoil hands AMD and Intel an opening, and the financing web is now run by the CFO who built it.

What to Know About Colette Kress (#26)

The interim leader is the company's longtime finance chief and a central architect of the vendor-financing arrangements that made Nvidia a lender to its own market.

Singapore Says U.S. Not Helpful on Suspected Spy's Company (#25)

Singapore authorities say Washington has not meaningfully cooperated with their investigation into Megaspeed, the data-center firm where Alice Huang was a top executive. A containment architecture built on allied chokepoints cannot function if evidence flows one way.

Home Sales Fall to a 16-Year Low (#21)

The 30-year fixed rate slipped to 7.11 percent from 7.18 percent — the fourth straight weekly decline, and still 87 basis points above a year ago. Existing-home sales fell 8.8 percent year over year and 5.5 percent from September, to a 3.75 million annual rate, the slowest since August 2010, against a healthy pace of about 5 million. "I don't think affordability challenges are going away any time soon," said Homes.com's Brad Case, "because I don't think we're likely to see substantially lower mortgage rates."

Fed Will Not Hold a Policy Vote at Its October 2027 Meeting (#24)

Chairman Kevin Warsh scrapped the December meeting and converted the Oct. 26-27, 2027 gathering into a strategy session with no vote and no statement. The Fed still meets eight times next year, but with no December session, rates will not be voted on between mid-November 2027 and Jan. 25-26, 2028. By 2028 the plan is six rate-setting meetings plus two strategy sessions. Warsh's stated aim is to "reserve time and space... for strategic deliberations." The timing is the problem: the Fed reduced its sampling rate the same week a single personnel decision moved a trillion dollars.

Tech Giants Invest in German Robotics Start-Up at $8.5 Billion (#22)

Agile Robots raised $500 million from SoftBank and Nvidia at an $8.5 billion valuation, $2 billion total raised, with close to 30,000 systems deployed including 3,000 to 4,000 humanoids. Founder Zhaopeng Chen frames it as Europe's answer: "Physical A.I. is one of the solutions for Europe."

United Airlines Blocks A.I. Agents From Booking Flights (#23)

United barred A.I. assistants from logging into accounts or booking flights, citing data exposure, "unintended purchases," account tampering, and specific regulatory impossibility — "flight check-in requires human acknowledgment that bags do not contain banned substances like explosives." Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta did not comment. This is the first major carrier to write the rule the regulators have not.

New York City Bans Surveillance Pricing (#34)

The Council voted 44-5 to outlaw algorithmic price-setting based on spending habits, demographics or other factors, with a $1,000 fine per violation and — unlike the state bill awaiting the governor's signature — an appeal right for small businesses. Mayor Zohran Mamdani: "New Yorkers' personal data should never be used as a weapon against their own pocketbooks." Connecticut, Maryland, New Jersey and Seattle have already moved.

Where Did Milei's Revolution Go? (#33)

Argentina's libertarian experiment has hit the limits of austerity — rising prices, an exhausted middle class, and a Silicon Valley alliance that has not delivered.

Prediction

Existing-home sales will remain below a 4.2 million annual rate through the first quarter of 2027. (Rationale: the 16-year low of 3.75 million reflects a rate-level constraint at 7.11 percent, not sentiment; four weeks of 7-18 basis-point declines cannot restore affordability from 87 basis points above last year, and locked-in owners suppress listings that would otherwise normalize the market.) Rescue Ends in Brooklyn After 8 Die (#3)

The first American-born pontiff flew from Buenos Aires to Lima — not to Chicago — less than a month after surgeons removed a lobe of his right lung, and called Peru "my homeland." About three million people lined the route. He was greeted by President Keiko Fujimori, whose father's policies he once protested. Judge Richard A. Posner Has Died (#27)

The most-cited legal scholar of the second half of the 20th century died Tuesday in Chicago at 87. He wrote thousands of opinions over 36 years on the Seventh Circuit and more than 50 books; a 2000 study found he out-cited Ronald Dworkin by nearly two to one. Cluster 5: Science, Health & Environment

Thematic Hook

Two American designations are expiring this month, and both were earned over decades and spent in two years.

The U.S. Has Lost Its Measles-Elimination Status (#17)

Genetic analysis conducted at the Times's request shows measles is again endemic, 26 years after elimination was certified in 2000. Nearly 5,200 cases have been reported this year; eight deaths, including one each reported last week by Pennsylvania, New York and Wisconsin — more than the combined toll from 2000 through 2024. The legal test is uninterrupted transmission for more than 12 months, and several chains have already run that long: two in Utah and one in Arizona active in July 2026 all trace to a Texas outbreak. A PAHO committee convenes Tuesday; the decision is expected Nov. 17.

When Measles Came to an Amish Community (#35)

Lewis County, N.Y., offers the local anatomy of the national number — an under-vaccinated community, dozens hospitalized, one dead.

A Super El Niño Is Bearing Down. "What Are We to Do?" (#12)

Farmers in Namasoko, Malawi, were summoned under the village's largest wild mango tree and told to buy drought-resistant seed at $43 a bag. Most earn under $3 a day. Two forces raised the price of coping: the U.S. war in Iran has made fertilizer and fuel much costlier, and aid cuts have pushed sub-Saharan funding to its lowest level since the early 2000s. The UN's 22-country El Niño plan has not started work in Malawi and six other nations for lack of money, and planting season begins now.

Prostate Cancer Screening Now Broadly Recommended (#36)

New guidance reverses years of emphasis on shared decision-making and broadly recommends universal screening — one of the few health stories this week moving toward more intervention rather than less.

Prediction

The PAHO committee will announce on Nov. 17 that the United States has lost measles-elimination status. (Rationale: the test is durational, not discretionary — multiple transmission chains have exceeded 12 months on public genomic data, three states reported measles deaths in a single week, and the panel's only question is whether the documented chains meet a threshold that the published evidence already satisfies.) 翟东升 (Zhai Dongsheng) — CDP Fellow, Renmin University

一、底层逻辑：这不是一个人的道德问题，是主权信用的定价问题

黄仁勋辞职这件事，绝大多数人看的是丑闻，我看到的是定价机制。英伟达占先进AI芯片九成份额，市值以万亿计，又是整个行业的"影子央行"——用自己的资产负债表给客户融资。这种结构在金融史上有一个专门的名字：单点故障。一个六十三岁的创始人，没有任何继任安排，董事会开几个小时会才想起让CFO顶上。这不是治理，这是消防演习。

真正值得看的是同一天十年期美债收益率破5%。按常理，科技股崩盘应该推动资金避险买国债，收益率该往下走。它反而往上走了。这说明市场不是在定价"衰退"，而是在定价信用——当美国最有价值公司的领导层可以在一夜之间蒸发一万亿美元，这个国家的制度可预期性就被重新折价了。美元不是商品货币，是主权信用货币，它的价值来自法律可执行性、军事投射能力和政治稳定。这三根柱子都在系统性钙化。

二、结构性两难：美联储在降低采样率

沃什把2027年12月那次议息会议直接取消，还把一个会议改成不投票的战略讨论。到2028年只剩六次利率决策。他的原话是要"给战略思考留出时间和空间"。听起来很有智慧，但请注意时点：美联储宣布减少决策次数的同一周，一家公司的一个人事决定就推动了一万亿美元和5%的国债收益率。在系统波动率上升的时候降低控制回路的采样频率，这是结构性两难，不是政策选择。

三、平行体系：盟友的裂缝比对手的进攻更致命

还有一个细节没人注意：新加坡公开说，美国没有实质配合他们对Megaspeed的调查。芯片管制这套体系，靠的是新加坡、荷兰、日本这些咽喉点的统一执法。现在咽喉点说自己拿不到证据。这不是中美的问题，是美国联盟体系内部信息互信的问题。 美元环流靠的是信用，信用靠的是信息对称。信息一旦单向流动，平行体系就不是中国推出来的，是美国自己让出来的。

一句话：英伟达跌的是股价，美国跌的是制度溢价。

金灿荣 (Jin Canrong) — Professor, Renmin University

一、今天的头版很有意思

各位朋友，今天这份报纸看下来，我第一句话是：美国人现在很焦虑。

你看这个顺序啊。头版第一条，英伟达的老总黄仁勋辞职了，因为跟下属有私情，还跟一个美国官员说是中国间谍的女人有私情，一个月市值蒸发一万亿美元。第二条，同一个女人，讲中国的情报工作怎么用感情做长线。第三条，布鲁克林一栋楼塌了，八个人死了。第五条，十年期国债收益率破5%。

一个国家的头版如果全是这种事，说明什么？说明它内部的管理成本已经高到压不住了。

二、这个事情其实很简单

特朗普把万斯从伊朗谈判里撤回来，让他去查民主党城市的"舞弊"。你们记不记得2020年那会儿？也是这个套路。这就像一个饭店老板，菜还没炒好，先把桌子掀了。 掀桌子不是本事，是没本事。

为什么？因为中期选举输得太难看。八个参议院席位翻盘，七个是在2012年以后民主党总统选举从没赢过的州；德克萨斯州级职位全丢；印第安纳都丢了两个。输了怎么办？不能承认政策有问题，那就换个战场——不比政策，比谁有资格计数。这是典型的"制度内程序，目的倒过来用"。

三、工业本体论：谁在造东西，谁说话算数

我这里要提醒一句。黄仁勋这件事，很多人当成八卦看，其实它暴露的是美国AI繁荣的空心化——九成芯片份额，靠一家公司；这一家的融资，靠它自己的资产负债表给客户垫钱；这个人一走，一万亿美元就没了。这是什么？这是把整个国家的增长押在一个人身上。

反过来看乌克兰那一条，五百万段战场视频，开放给盟国公司免费用，条件是用出来的软件要给乌军授权。这才是真正的战争资源——数据。 但乌克兰为什么要卖？因为它缺兵、缺防空导弹。工业能力不够，就只能拿数据换武器。

四、结论

中美竞争现在是战略相持阶段，互有攻守。美国的问题不是中国太强，是它自己的工业化空心化，B-52从1955年飞到现在还在飞。

我们这边要做的，还是那句话：把自己的事情做好。 芯片、能源、粮食，一样一样补齐。外面越乱，越要稳坐钓鱼台。

这就叫百年未有之大变局——东升西降，不是口号，是账本。

宋
宋鸿兵 (Song Hongbing) — Author, Currency Wars

一、如果你把AI繁荣看成一个电路，它的单点故障在哪里

我这些年一直讲一个方法：任何系统都有单点故障，找到它，你就理解了整个系统。

今天这份报纸把这个单点故障画得很清楚。英伟达控制九成先进AI芯片，同时又是行业的"影子央行"——用复杂的协议给自己的客户和供应商融资。这就是美元环流在企业层面的复制品：钱从上游流到下游，再以订单和应收账款的形式流回来。 而这个回路的阀门，握在一个人手里。他辞职那天，一个月蒸发的一万亿美元，加上十年期美债收益率破5%，就是这个回路短路的声音。

请注意一个反常现象：科技股大跌，资金没有去买国债避险，收益率反而上去了。这不是股市的问题，是整个系统的问题。 说明市场在给美国的制度信用重新定价，而不是在给增长重新定价。

二、金融高边疆：芯片管制是一套清算体系

美国对中国的芯片管制，本质上不是贸易政策，是金融高边疆的延伸——它靠的是对新加坡、荷兰、日本这些咽喉点的统一执法，跟SWIFT的运作逻辑一模一样。

但今天有一条新闻，分量比英伟达还重：新加坡说，美国没有实质配合他们对Megaspeed的调查。 这是什么？这是清算体系内部的信息不对称。一套管制体系，如果连自己的节点都拿不到证据，那它就不是执法，是自觉。而自觉在金融史上是靠不住的——十九世纪上海的外资银行之所以能控制中国的经济命脉，不是因为它们更强，是因为它们掌握了清算。

三、三变六反：这是第四轮货币 regime 转换的中段

我讲过"三变六反"：全球化反转为本土化，美元霸权反转为多极储备，美国主导秩序反转为平行体系。现在处在"六反"的深化阶段——每一个后冷战时代的假设都在倒过来。

住房那一条是同一件事的另一面。三十年期房贷7.11%，比一年前高87个基点；成屋销售375万套年化，是2010年8月以来最低。这不是利率问题，是美元的购买力在从资产端向负债端转移。谁手里有资产谁被锁住，谁没资产谁买不起。这种结构在货币史上每一次出现，后面都跟着一次财富再分配。

四、结论

三件事连起来看：一个CEO能动摇一万亿，一个盟友能掏空一套管制，一个利率能锁死一个住房市场。 这不是三个问题，这是同一个问题的三个切面——美国的系统冗余度已经被抽干了。
